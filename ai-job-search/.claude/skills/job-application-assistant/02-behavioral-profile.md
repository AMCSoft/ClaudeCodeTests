## Overview
Angelo Marino Carmollingo self-assesses as a **persistent deep-problem engineer with a facilitation
habit**: energised by hard technical problems and by movement, drained by repetitive and sterile
work. Two years as Scrum Master and three months as Project Manager / Technical Lead sit alongside
the hands-on engineering rather than replacing it - the profile is a senior individual contributor
who also runs the process, not a manager stepping away from the code.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Problem persistence | High | Stays with hard problems for days or weeks; self-described as going "beyond my limits every day" |
| Need for variety / pace | High | Performs best in dynamic environments; stagnation and routine are the main demotivators |
| Team orientation | High | Works well in teams and has actively taken facilitation roles (Scrum Master, Technical Lead) |
| Tolerance for routine process work | Low | Repetitive, sterile tasks - documentation being the named example - actively drain energy |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Sticks with hard problems:** does not abandon a problem that resists for days or weeks. The solo spacecraft-filesystem thesis is the strongest evidence.
- **Facilitates without being asked to lead:** two years running SCRUM ceremonies as Scrum Master, then stepped up to Project Manager / Technical Lead - the team-process role came in addition to the engineering work, not instead of it.
- **Learns unfamiliar deep stacks fast:** AUTOSAR, spacecraft memory systems, V2V, OpenGL - each was entered cold and became productive work.

## How You Work Best
- Dynamic environments where the problem set changes and there is room to move
- Hands-on technical ownership of a component, end to end
- Team settings with clear process - comfortable both inside the ceremonies and running them
- Work where the result is measurable, not a matter of taste

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Low tolerance for documentation and repetitive process work:** frame as a bias toward automating or tooling around the repetitive part rather than as an unwillingness to do it - which is literally the track record (Python test automation around the C codebase at KooSys). Never phrase this as "I don't like writing documentation" in an application: in regulated sectors it reads as disqualifying.
- **Leadership experience is recent and short:** three months as PM / Technical Lead is real but thin. Claim the Scrum Master track record (two years) confidently and the PM/Tech Lead role honestly as recent.
- **Cloud, distributed systems and Rust are absent from production experience:** frame as the deliberate next step, backed by the systems fundamentals already in place.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "fast-moving", "fast-paced", "dynamic environment", "bleeding edge"
- "ownership", "ownership-driven", "end-to-end responsibility", "autonomy"
- "hard problems", "deep technical challenges", "performance-critical"
- "Agile", "SCRUM", "cross-functional team", "collaborative"
- "continuous improvement", "learning new technologies"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "extensive documentation", "documentation-heavy", "technical writing a core responsibility"
- "certification", "compliance", "audit", "regulatory validation" as the *bulk* of the role rather than a constraint on it
- "maintenance of legacy systems", "support role", "ticket-driven"
- "highly structured", "well-established processes", "stable, mature codebase" with no mention of new work
- "waterfall", "fixed specification"

> **Known tension, worth naming:** the aerospace and defense direction is a strong *skills* match
> (flight-grade ISO-C99, PUS, validation) and a weak *motivation* match - that sector is the most
> documentation- and certification-heavy there is, and documentation is the named energy drain.
> When scoring an aerospace posting, weigh Motivation deliberately rather than letting the skills
> score carry it.
